Machine learning · Climate research

High-resolution NO₂ mapping

Tracking global NO₂ levels matters for understanding air quality and climate change, but generating high-resolution measurements with WRF-CHEM is computationally intensive. With UC Berkeley’s Cohen Research Group, I helped lay the groundwork for a machine-learning alternative.

I aligned more than 47,000 WRF-CHEM pixels with NASA OMI pixels and engineered geolocation data to improve correlation, streamline analysis, and prepare the dataset for model training.

Natural language processing · Deep learning

Part-of-speech tagging with BiLSTMs

Part-of-speech tagging identifies the grammatical role of each word in a sentence, giving language systems essential context. I built a tagger with a bidirectional LSTM trained using mini-batch stochastic gradient descent and pretrained GloVe embeddings.

I incorporated dropout to reduce overfitting and experimented with model configurations using the 500,000 most frequent 300-dimensional word embeddings.

Sociolinguistics · UC Berkeley

How people say “yes” and “no”

People in the same linguistic community can communicate the same idea in strikingly different ways. I studied variation in confirmation and declination responses among English speakers at UC Berkeley.

Over two weeks, I collected auditory linguistic data and analyzed how response variants related to gender, race, relationship formality, and situational context. The experience connected quantitative analysis with the social texture behind the data.
